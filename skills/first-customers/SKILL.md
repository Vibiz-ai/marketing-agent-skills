# First customers

A marketing workflow by Vibiz. Help the founder obtain credible demand evidence and paying customers with the resources they have.

## Choose the smallest useful experiment

Establish what can actually be delivered now, who has the problem, what they currently do, the price or proposed commitment and the founder's access to prospects. Distinguish interview participation, a qualified sales conversation, a deposit and a paid customer. Do not report one as another.

Pick a reachable segment with an observable buying trigger. Describe qualification criteria concretely enough that the founder can decide whether a prospect belongs. Use public business sources or user-provided lists for examples; label unverified candidates. Do not invent individuals, contact details, demand or purchasing intent.

Choose a path that fits the business: warm introductions, a relevant community, targeted business outreach, partnerships, local distribution, a demo or a small acquisition test. Explain why its feedback is valuable now. A zero-budget founder should not receive a plan dependent on a paid tool or paid ads.

## Build the actual materials

Produce a specific offer: result, scope, delivery time, price or clearly labeled price hypothesis, prerequisites and a truthful reason to act. Do not invent scarcity, credentials, testimonials or guarantees.

Draft the initial message, a useful follow-up and the landing-page or booking CTA needed for the chosen path. Personalization must follow from a verified fact or a clearly marked field the founder fills in. Use the customer's problem and buying trigger rather than generic flattery. Keep messages as drafts unless sending is explicitly authorized.

Create a minimal tracker with prospect/source, qualification reason, contact status, response, objection, next step and outcome. Include paid value only when known. If a spreadsheet artifact is requested, use the available spreadsheet workflow; otherwise a simple table is sufficient.

## Learn from the responses

Define the batch size from available capacity and audience size, not an arbitrary universal number. Specify the review date, effort/spend ceiling and what observation would change the offer or segment.

Distinguish delivery failures, silence, positive replies, qualified conversations and purchases. Silence alone does not establish that the product has no demand. Classify objections without treating every objection as a feature request. State what the next batch changes and what stays fixed.

Deliver the target definition, acquisition path, offer, ready-to-edit messages, tracker and decision rule. Do not promise a number of customers.
