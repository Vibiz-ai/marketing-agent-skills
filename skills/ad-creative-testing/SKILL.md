# Ad creative testing

A marketing workflow by Vibiz. Deliver distinct concepts and an interpretable test; generating images is optional unless requested.

## Ground the brief

Read the offer, buyer, conversion action, destination, brand constraints and available evidence. Inspect existing assets or performance data when supplied. Establish the target platform, placement, language, market, budget ceiling and production capacity. If the offer or destination is unclear, resolve that before producing polished variations.

Separate the audience insight, angle, hook, proof and visual execution. Changing a headline's wording is not necessarily a new concept. Test meaningfully different explanations of why the buyer should act: a demonstrated result, the cost of the current workaround, an objection answered or a product mechanism. Use only claims the business can substantiate.

## Produce usable concept cards

For each concept, give the customer insight, promise, proof requirement, opening hook, visual direction, primary copy, headline, CTA and destination. For video, add a short sequence of shots, spoken copy, on-screen text and required source footage. For images, identify the focal point, product/brand assets and text hierarchy.

Explain what distinguishes the concepts and what each is intended to teach. Match the quantity to the budget and production bandwidth. Avoid a large matrix whose cells cannot each receive a meaningful test. Label stock, synthetic or example material accurately; do not invent customer testimonials or imply generated people are real customers.

## Design an interpretable test

Choose the business conversion event and a diagnostic metric for each funnel step. Keep audience, offer and destination stable where feasible when comparing creative concepts. Identify confounders when that is impossible, including uneven algorithmic delivery.

Set a total spend ceiling, duration or review window, operational stop conditions and the evidence needed for a follow-up. If there is insufficient data to compare business outcomes, say so. CTR alone does not establish profitable acquisition; a zero-conversion test does not prove a loser without considering sample size and the conversion window.

Do not recommend larger spending merely to obtain significance. Offer a cheaper qualitative or sequential test when the budget cannot answer the question. Use observed economics to set thresholds; otherwise label assumptions.

Return the concept cards, production checklist and test table. Separate proposed experiments from observed results and prepared assets from launched campaigns.
