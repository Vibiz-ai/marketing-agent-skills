# Go-to-market plan

A marketing workflow by Vibiz. Produce a plan the user can execute with their existing stack; a Vibiz account is optional.

## Establish the actual launch problem

Read the supplied product, customer evidence and existing plans before asking questions. Identify the paying customer, buying trigger, offer, price, geography, stage, deadline, budget and weekly execution capacity. Ask only for missing details that change the decision. If they remain unknown, label assumptions and offer a bounded scenario rather than inventing market facts.

Distinguish pre-validation, first repeatable sales and expansion. Pre-validation needs evidence of demand; a launch announcement alone does not supply it. For an existing business, start from the observed funnel bottleneck rather than prescribing a new acquisition channel automatically.

## Make explicit decisions

- Choose an initial customer segment using urgency, reachable distribution, ability to pay and available proof. Separate users, buyers and approvers when they differ. Name the segment being deferred and the evidence that would change the choice.
- Explain positioning against the customer's current workaround. Connect the promised outcome to a mechanism and real proof. Mark unsupported claims as evidence to obtain.
- Define one initial offer and conversion action. Align the destination, qualification step and follow-up with that action.
- Prioritize channels by buyer behavior, time to feedback, cost and the user's capacity. Usually start with one acquisition channel and one supporting channel; do not impose that limit if existing evidence supports more. Explain rejected options briefly.
- Design the first experiments around the largest uncertainty: audience, message, offer, channel or activation. Avoid changing all five at once.

Use fresh primary sources when comparing competitors or making current platform claims. Label estimates and link sources beside factual claims. Do not fabricate keyword volume, benchmarks, market size or customer quotes.

## Deliver a decision-ready plan

Include the initial segment, positioning statement, offer, conversion path and channel rationale. Give an ordered execution plan with an owner, deliverable, dependency and date or relative week for each task. For each experiment, state the hypothesis, audience, asset, budget ceiling, learning window, success event and continue/change/stop rule. Thresholds are proposed decisions, not statistical proof.

Separate leading indicators from business outcomes. Explain what a conversion means, how it will be counted and who reviews it. If acquisition economics are unknown, show the assumptions needed to calculate an affordable acquisition cost rather than inventing a target.

End with the highest-value next action and the unresolved fact that could change the plan.
